Bürgeramt

Anmeldung and Meldebescheinigung: registering your address in Germany

Last reviewed: 25 October 20263 min read
In short

When you move into a flat in Germany, you must register your address at the registration office (Bürgeramt, Einwohnermeldeamt or Bürgerbüro) within two weeks. You need your passport or ID and a confirmation from your landlord (Wohnungsgeberbestätigung). You receive a registration certificate (Meldebescheinigung), which you will need for many other steps.

What is the Anmeldung?

The Anmeldung is the registration of your residential address with the local registration authority (Meldebehörde). Under the Federal Registration Act (Bundesmeldegesetz), anyone who moves into a home in Germany must register within two weeks of moving in. The office has different names depending on the city: Bürgeramt, Bürgerbüro, Einwohnermeldeamt or Bürgerservice.

Registration is a key first step: you need the Meldebescheinigung (registration certificate) for opening many bank accounts, for the Ausländerbehörde, for family benefits, for your employer and more. After your first registration in Germany, the Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern) sends your tax identification number (Steuer-ID) by post.

What to bring

  • Your passport or national ID card (and those of family members you register).
  • The Wohnungsgeberbestätigung — a confirmation from your landlord or the person who gives you the home that you moved in. The landlord is legally required to provide it.
  • The registration form (Anmeldeformular), if your city asks you to fill it in beforehand.
  • For family members: documents such as marriage or birth certificates may be requested — sometimes with an official translation. Check your city's website.
  • Your appointment confirmation (Terminbestätigung), if you booked online.

Appointments and letters

In many cities you need to book an appointment (Termin) online. You usually receive an email or letter with a Terminbestätigung (appointment confirmation) and sometimes a booking number (Vorgangsnummer). If you cannot get an appointment within two weeks, keep proof of your attempts — many offices understand this situation, but rules differ between cities.

At the appointment, you receive the Meldebescheinigung, usually printed on the spot. Check that names, date of birth and address are spelled correctly before you leave.

Letters that often follow

  • Your Steuer-ID from the Bundeszentralamt für Steuern — keep this letter safe.
  • A letter from the Beitragsservice about the broadcasting contribution (Rundfunkbeitrag).
  • If you move within Germany, you register at the new address (Ummeldung). If you leave Germany, you deregister (Abmeldung).

Not registering in time

Late registration is an administrative offence and can lead to a fine. More importantly, without a registration you may get stuck with other important steps. Book the appointment as soon as you have your landlord's confirmation.

What to do

  1. Get the WohnungsgeberbestätigungAsk your landlord for the confirmation as soon as you move in.
  2. Book an appointmentUse your city's online booking or call the office. Save the confirmation.
  3. Prepare documentsPassport or ID, landlord confirmation, form, and documents for family members.
  4. Attend and check the certificateMake sure all details on the Meldebescheinigung are correct.
  5. Keep the letters that followEspecially the Steuer-ID letter — you will need it for work and taxes.

Useful German phrases

  • Ich möchte meinen Wohnsitz anmelden.I would like to register my address.
  • Ich bin am … eingezogen.I moved in on …
  • Hier ist die Wohnungsgeberbestätigung.Here is the landlord's confirmation.
  • Ich habe leider keinen früheren Termin bekommen.Unfortunately I could not get an earlier appointment.
  • Könnten Sie mir bitte eine Meldebescheinigung ausdrucken?Could you please print a registration certificate for me?

Where to get free or low-cost help

  • Migrationsberatung für erwachsene Zuwanderer (MBE)Free, confidential counselling for immigrants aged 27 and over, offered by welfare organisations with federal funding. Counsellors help you understand letters, fill in forms and contact authorities. Find an office near you with the BAMF-NAvI search.
  • Jugendmigrationsdienste (JMD)Free counselling for young people with a migration background, generally aged 12 to 27 — school, training, work and dealing with authorities.
